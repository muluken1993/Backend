1. INTRODUCTION 
A bachelor’s thesis is the first academic thesis. Its purpose is to develop the basic 
skills of academic research. After completion of bachelor’s thesis, the graduate will be 
familiar with the methods and tools of academic information retrieval and will be able 
to critically evaluate the reliability and significance of published information. The end 
product is a thesis that adheres to the general principles of academic writing and 
which you will present orally in a seminar. Academic writing has its own particular style, with the emphasis on the subject matter 
along with precision of expression and the use of grammatically correct language. In 
addition, academic texts follow rules and conventions that have been generally agreed 
upon. This manual presents the principles of academic writing as well as the formal 
writing requirements for thesis in EiTEX. 2. THESIS WRITING PROCESSES 
2. 1. Selecting Research Topic 
However, writing a thesis usually progresses according to the following steps. The 
process starts with the choice of the research topic, proposed by the graduate and will 
be discussed together with the advisor. The research topic for the thesis can be found 
in any subject area of the field. A thesis may also be commissioned by a company or 
other organization outside the university. As in all decisions in scientific research, there must be solid arguments concerning the 
choice of the research topic. There are several aspects worth much: 
➔ personal interest in the topic 
➔ own resources (knowledge, skills and time available) 
➔ Availability of source material 
➔ Data availability 
➔ Scientific importance of the topic 
➔ General social interest of the topic and 
➔ Importance of the topic for your future professional career. 2 
 
However, students shall form a group of three student members and submit thesis 
topics with reserve titles along with each topic description. Then, the respective 
program head will assign committee responsible to select the appropriate title. 2. 2. Literature Review 
After the topic has been chosen and the advisor has accepted it, the next step is 
gaining familiarity with the available scientific literature on the topic. Reading earlier 
research serves several purposes: 
i. Outlining the general picture 
- What is the state-of-the-art knowledge in the chosen research area? Is there much 
or little earlier research? Is it recent or older? Who are the leading scholars? - From which viewpoints has the topic been investigated before? What kinds of 
methods and data have been used in earlier research? What are the most important 
findings? - How does your thesis relate to other publications in the field? ii. Strengthening the substance knowledge 
- Helps you to focus on the essential literature regarding your own thesis. - Helps you to choose the studies upon which you will develop the theoretical framework 
of the thesis and design its empirical research setting. iii. The development of expertise 
- Helps you to evaluate the existing literature critically and to identify potential gaps and 
shortcomings in it. - Helps you to make your viewpoint precise and to define the actual research problem or 
research task. The choice of research topic and getting acquainted with the literature in the chosen research 
area are of vital importance in getting the thesis process started. In order to ensure the smooth 
progress of the research process and in order to achieve successful results, narrowing down 
the research topic is the most critical task. 2. 3. The Purpose of the Research Proposal 
The purpose of the proposal is to clearly set out the following:

2. 4. 1. Proposal Content 
The next step in the process is writing a preliminary research plan. This document 
usually includes the following: 
➔ Preliminary title of the thesis 
➔ Preliminary table of contents of the thesis 
➔ Introduction of the topic: to arise arouse interest and convince the reader that the 
research is justifiable and the problem is worth researching. ➔ Justification and Problem Statement: Assessment of the significance of the 
topic and the motivation for the research 
➔ Objectives: General and Specific objectives describe the aim of the thesis and the 
research problem or task 
➔ Outline of preliminary research question(s) 
➔ Significance, Scopes and Limitations 
➔ Brief review of existing researches and Literatures: Including issues addressed, 
employed methods mentioning basic theories, conceptual frame works, employed 
data collection instruments and sampling as well as major gaps and identified 
research gaps. ➔ Brief description of the research method(s) and Materials: describe research 
approach and design, data collection instruments, population, sampling technique, 
sampling size, data analysis, data validity and reliability. ➔ Work plans: including both financial, material and time period 
➔ Preliminary list of references: Use the Harvard referencing style format. The preliminary research plan can simply be composed of a sketch of outlines for the 
thesis. It serves as a guide for you at the outset of your work. However, the plan will

2. 5. Progress Report 
Thesis work in EiTEX involves an intermediate report. This report is presented when 
one or more sections of the thesis, for example the theoretical framework, the 
literature review or, in case of data-driven research, after the data description and 
analysis have been finished. It is also recommended that the sketch an outline of the 
unfinished sections (Future Works) of the thesis, challenges, tasks covered against 
the work plan, etc will be presented in the intermediate report. 2. 5. 1 Progress Report Coverage 
Progress reports should report progresses. Some research students feel that they 
should report results, which is usually very different from progress. Researchers can 
show a completed literature survey, materials to be used, research methods along with 
pilot test to explicit data validity and reliability. Progress report will take place 
according to the schedule of the program head. The assigned juries will assess the 
extent of work the student has done and gives recommendations, corrections and 
directions to the remaining works to be done. 2. 5. 2. Progress Submission 
➔ After the students prepared the progress report with the consultation of the 
advisor, they are required to submit final soft copy of the report and power point 
slides along with one-page advisors declaration at least three days before the

of mid of the second semester of the final year to the respective program head 
office. ➔ The juries will evaluate the proposal according to the format given under Annex 
F. ➔ Each student shall present their own contribution and status of the work for 45 
minutes per topic (15 minutes for each student). 6
